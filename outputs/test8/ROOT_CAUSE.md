# Phase 4.5 root-cause audit

The Phase 4 checkpoint audit recomputed the gate on all 540 traces across 90 training windows. All decisions matched the stored results. Six passed, 122 had unavailable concentration, and 412 had sufficient processed coverage and scored below 0.35. Among the 418 scorable traces, the median spectral concentration was 0.119 and the maximum was 0.426. Of the 122 unscorable traces, 108 were from ROI-windows with zero snapped RGB coverage and 14 had some snapped RGB but no valid concentration. Missing observations limited coverage, while low concentration was the dominant direct reason for rejection. See [the replayed gate decisions](phase4_pilot_gate_audit.csv).

| Audit area | Finding and limits |
| --- | --- |
| RGB/BGR | OpenCV decodes BGR. Both samplers reverse channels before reporting RGB. The MediaPipe face crop is explicitly converted to RGB. A regression checks asymmetric BGR pixel values. |
| POS | The projections of mean-normalized channels are G−B and G+B−2R, combined with their standard deviation ratio. The existing overlap-count normalization and zero-spread guard are implementation choices. A single-window regression verifies the algebra. [Wang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TBME.2016.2609282) |
| Timestamp and FPS | The baseline uses monotonic decoder positions and inverse median frame interval. Phase 4 rounds analysis FPS to 12 decimal places. CFR fallback requires opt-in. The inventory compares duration, frame count and FPS with container metadata. The FFmpeg PTS probe checks two representations within one backend. Camera clock accuracy remains unknown. |
| Resampling and missing observations | The legacy continuous export can interpolate across gaps up to 0.25 s. Phase 4 snaps within one-quarter sample period, discards collisions and does no filling. Unique run IDs retain ROI source changes even if a switch is off-grid. One missing grid sample can break a run and leave observations below the four-second processing minimum. Bounded interpolation is tested separately. |
| Detrending and bandpass | The baseline detrends linearly, applies a third-order 0.7–3.0 Hz Butterworth bandpass forwards and backwards per valid run, then z-scores. Forward-backward filtering affects edges and magnitude response. Z-scoring removes absolute amplitude. The four-second minimum uses a floating-point ceiling, so slightly elevated FPS near an integer boundary can require one extra sample. Pulse release additionally requires six contiguous seconds and a passing longest run. |
| ROI stability and skin | The hybrid follows a face box and landmarks, smooths small shape changes and constrains fallback to nearby eligible pixels. Geometry, exposure and motion can still change the observed skin patch. The baseline excludes facial features and exposure extremes. A chroma mask can reduce contamination and also discard darker or white-balance-shifted skin. |
| Motion and illumination | Face-center displacement and background luminance variation include tracking noise and scene changes. The 2D rigid compensation cannot correct out-of-plane motion or facial deformation. Background scaling can inject variation if the background differs from face illumination. The extra motion-aware rejection rule leaves the 0.35 gate in place. |
| Compression | The inputs are nominal C23 H.264. Container bits per pixel per frame mixes scene content, motion, resolution and encoding decisions. Generated textured frames isolate codec effects on known inputs. The FF++ clips in this experiment lack synchronized reference pulse and paired lossless footage, leaving their compression contribution unidentified. [Controlled compression study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7899506/) |
| Spectral concentration | The numerator uses pre-bandpass Welch power within ±one bin of the strongest in-band peak, clipped to the band. The denominator sums non-DC power, including out-of-band power. The metric rewards narrow peaks from any signal source and penalizes broader or changing pulse content. Shorter valid runs have wider bins. Welch caps its segment length at eight seconds. In-band/out-of-band power is a descriptive proxy. |
| Duration | Longer windows can support more cycles and Welch averages. They can also pool nonstationary content, contain short valid runs, or exclude short videos. The paired duration analysis uses nested time intervals of the same video and ROI, with an additional subset having at least 95% processed coverage at both durations. |

On the eight-second baseline, a single four-second processed run already supplies about half the window. That makes the coverage condition largely redundant once concentration is available in the pilot. It matters more for longer windows, where four seconds can yield a score without meeting the 50% requirement.

The evidence does not identify a gross channel-order, POS algebra or gate-comparison bug. The selected videos measure pipeline behavior on compressed footage. The contributions of genuinely weak pulse, nuisance variation and compression cannot be separated without synchronized ground truth.

## Algorithm formulations

- **GREEN** uses the green-channel spatial mean. The shared analysis path detrends, filters and normalizes the trace. [Verkruysse et al.](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.16.021434)
- **POS** uses the baseline 1.6-second overlapping projections above. [Wang et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/TBME.2016.2609282)
- **CHROM** mean-normalizes RGB in 1.6-second windows, forms X = 3R−2G and Y = 1.5R+G−1.5B, and estimates X−αY with α computed from bandpassed projection standard deviations. Half-overlap tapered synthesis uses a weight sum to normalize edge gain. The main comparison scores the unfiltered synthesis with that α. The filtered-synthesis diagnostic has a different measurement basis because it filters the denominator before scoring. [De Haan and Jeanne](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23744659/)
- **ICA** uses the blind source separation approach of Poh et al. This variant detrends and standardizes RGB, whitens through SVD, fits symmetric tanh FastICA with deterministic initialization, and chooses a component by its strongest in-band Welch peak relative to non-DC power. It leaves nonconvergent runs missing. Solver and component selection differ from the original experiment. [Poh et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20588929/), [Hyvärinen and Oja](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0893-6080(00)00026-5)
- **LGI** forms I−uuᵀ from the leading singular direction of the RGB trajectory and uses the green coordinate of the projection. The experiment fits the nuisance direction once per contiguous window-local run. [Pilz et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018_workshops/papers/w27/Pilz_Local_Group_Invariance_CVPR_2018_paper.pdf)

The methods were implemented from mathematical descriptions. All five use the shared scoring path with the original concentration and coverage thresholds. Cross-ROI absolute correlation and frequency disagreement use the longest joint processed run. Both describe candidate signal consistency, which can arise from motion or illumination shared across ROIs.
